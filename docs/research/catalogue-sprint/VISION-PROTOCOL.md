# Vision extraction: experiment results and protocol

## What was tested (2026-10-14)
- **Test set:** 3 synthetic release posters, generated by `research/catalogue-engine/tools/vision_bench/make_posters.py`. They use no third-party images.
  - 27 pins, each with a caption (subject), French edition text ("Édition limitée 800") and a € price.
  - Poster 3 is rotated, blurred and down-scaled.
- **Readers:** two independent vision readers, blind to the answers. Reader A is a full model; reader B is a small, fast model.
- **Scoring:** `tools/vision_bench/score.py` matches by position and requires exact field values.

| Reader | Pins found | Fields correct | Time per poster |
|---|---|---|---|
| A (full model) | 27/27 | 108/108 (100%) | ≈ 8 s |
| B (small model) | 27/27 | 108/108 (100%) | ≈ 6 s |
| A vs B agreement | | 108/108 agree; 0 agreed-but-wrong | |

**What this does and does not show**
- **Shows:** reading **printed text** on a multi-pin poster (subject captions, edition sizes, prices, French labels, mild skew and blur) is reliable and fast. The cheap model is good enough for this.
- **Does not show:** recognising a pin **from its artwork alone** (no caption), or reading real posters (glare, angles, small print, overlapping pins). Synthetic posters are a ceiling, not a field result.
- **Cross-checking:** two-model agreement is a useful gate. When two independent readers disagree, a person looks. In this test they never disagreed, so its error-catching value is not yet measured.

## Protocol for a real test (needs the maintainer's own photos)
1. **Photos:** take 5–10 photos yourself:
   - DLP monthly release posters or Pueblo Trading Post signage
   - Disney Store shelf tags or set backing cards
   - your own pins' backstamps

   Your own photos are permitted. No third-party images.
2. **Upload:** put them in a private folder, not the public repo. Tell Claude where.
3. **Extract:** Claude runs both readers. Results become a review sheet: one row per pin, with poster position, subject, edition and price.
4. **Check:** you mark each row correct or wrong in about 5 minutes. That gives real accuracy, plus the disagreement-catch rate.
5. **Decide:** if accuracy is high, poster capture becomes the DLP official-evidence route.

## How extracted pins would enter the catalogue (design, not built)
- **Extraction only proposes candidates.** It never verifies.
- **The photo is the source.** A person reviewing their own photo against the extracted rows is a direct check of official signage.
- **Loading is blocked by schema.** Recording these needs either a stored-photo source reference or a new evidence method (e.g. `photo_capture`). That is a schema change: a new migration, so maintainer approval.
- **Until then:** extracted rows stay in research packets, labelled as image extraction, and never count as evidence.
